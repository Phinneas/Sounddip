A group sound bath puts you in a room with strangers, all moving through the same sixty minutes of gong and bowl tones at the same pace. A private sound bath flips that. The practitioner works for you alone, or for the small group you bring, and the session bends around your needs instead of the room's average.

That changes three things in practice. The practitioner picks instruments based on what you tell them, not a generic playlist built for a crowd. You get more time to settle in and more time afterward to come back to yourself, since nobody else is waiting for the room. And you set the intention out loud before the session starts, which shapes the pacing and the instrument choices in real time.

## The three formats

Solo sessions are one practitioner and one client. This is the format most people mean when they say "private sound bath." It runs anywhere from 45 minutes to 90 minutes, usually in the practitioner's studio or your home, and it's built around a single conversation about what you want from the hour.

Private group sessions are a block booking: you reserve the practitioner's time for your friends, your book club, your birthday, your bachelorette weekend. The practitioner still runs one session, but for six or ten or fifteen people instead of one. Pricing is usually per person with a minimum headcount, so a group of six might pay less per head than a group of two.

Corporate sessions are private group sessions aimed at a workplace. Companies book these for offsites, wellness weeks, or as a one-off perk. The practitioner usually adjusts the format for an office setting: shorter runtime, chairs instead of mats, and a version of the session that doesn't require anyone to lie on the floor in work clothes.

## What it costs

Solo sessions typically run $75 to $250, depending on length, the practitioner's experience, and whether they travel to you. Expect the lower end for a 45-minute studio session and the higher end for a 90-minute in-home session with a well-known practitioner.

Private group sessions usually move to a per-person rate once you clear a minimum group size, often landing between $40 and $90 per person. Some practitioners charge a flat rate for the room plus a per-head add-on past a set number of guests.

Corporate bookings are priced case by case. Expect a flat day rate or per-session rate that reflects travel, equipment transport, and the practitioner blocking out their calendar for you. Ask for a quote directly rather than guessing from group rates, since the scope varies a lot between a 30-minute conference room session and a half-day wellness event.

## What to tell the practitioner before you book

Any serious practitioner will ask a few questions before confirming your session, and you'll get more out of the hour if you answer them honestly. Mention any health conditions that matter in a loud, vibration-heavy room: pregnancy, epilepsy, pacemakers, recent surgery, or sound sensitivity. Say whether you've done a sound bath before, since first-timers usually get a short explanation of what to expect before the instruments start. And say what you're actually hoping for. Sleep, stress, grief, a reset after a hard stretch, or just curiosity are all fine answers, and they change how the practitioner builds the session.

## What to ask when you book

Four questions cover most of what matters. Ask what instruments they use, since a gong-heavy session feels very different from one built around crystal bowls. Ask whether you'll be lying down or seated, so you know whether to bring a mat or wear something you can stretch out in. Ask about their cancellation policy, since private sessions often require more notice than a drop-in class. And for group or corporate bookings, ask what space requirements they have: ceiling height, floor space, outlets, and quiet.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a private sound bath worth the extra cost?

If you want a session built around a specific intention, more room to settle in, or you're sensitive to being in a group setting, yes. If you're just curious about sound baths generally, a group class is a cheaper way to find out first.

### Can I book a private sound bath at home?

Many practitioners travel to a client's home for solo and small group sessions, often for an added travel fee. Ask when you book, since not everyone offers this.

### How far in advance should I book?

Two to three weeks is a safe window for solo sessions. Group and corporate bookings often need four weeks or more, especially if the practitioner needs to coordinate space or bring extra equipment.

### Do private sessions include integration time afterward?

Most do, and it's one of the main advantages over a group class. Ask the practitioner how much time they build in after the sound portion ends.
