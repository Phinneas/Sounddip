Los Angeles has more sound baths per square mile than almost any city in the country, and the range is wide. On one end are practitioners who trained for years, sometimes overseas, and treat the gong or the bowl as a genuine clinical tool. On the other end are drop-in classes built mostly for the photo. Both have a place here. The hard part is knowing which is which before you book. This guide skips the exhaustive directory approach and picks seven sessions worth your time and money, chosen for what makes each one distinct rather than how many five-star reviews it has racked up. For the full list of LA listings, see the [LA directory page](/cities/los-angeles/).

LA's sound bath scene splits along geography as much as style. Silver Lake and Eagle Rock lean toward the walk-up studio model, where foot traffic and neighborhood loyalty sustain a practice over years without much paid marketing. Venice and Santa Monica lean toward the ocean itself, with sessions that use the beach or open water as part of the instrument, not just the backdrop for a nice photo. The Valley, in Tarzana and Woodland Hills, tends to be quieter, less saturated, and often noticeably cheaper than anything west of the 405. Westside neighborhoods like Brentwood and Westchester attract a more clinical, multi-modality crowd that treats sound as one piece of a broader wellness routine rather than the whole session. Knowing which of these you are looking for narrows the list fast.

## Sound Bath Los Angeles / One Conduit, in Hollywood

Carlina Baldonado Sullivan runs this practice out of Hollywood, pairing crystal bowls with gong across group, private, and corporate formats. The gong supplies the low end, a deep foundation tone, while the crystal bowls sit on top with more clarity and higher harmonics. What sets this one apart is reach: the practice travels to Santa Barbara, Ojai, Ventura, Malibu, Beverly Hills, West Hollywood, Santa Monica, and Pasadena, so it works as well for a corporate offsite as it does for a living room session. It holds a perfect rating across six reviews so far. Best for groups that want a mobile practitioner rather than a fixed studio address. Book through soundbathla.com.

## Shakti Sound Bath, in Brentwood

Ana Netanel has been doing this for more than two decades, and it shows in how the sessions are structured. Her work blends sound bath and sound healing with Kundalini Yoga, adding a devotional, energetic layer that goes beyond a passive lie-down-and-listen format. She has built a strong following: a 4.6 rating across 37 reviews, and a client base spread across Brentwood, Santa Monica, West LA, Malibu, and WeHo. Best for anyone who wants sound work paired with an actual yoga lineage instead of a borrowed aesthetic tacked onto a generic class. Public events are listed on Eventbrite, which makes it easy to see what is coming up before committing to a date.

## The Soundbath Center, in Eagle Rock

Jamie Bechtold has hosted more than 4,000 public sessions since 2015, which makes this arguably the most established gong practice in the city. The format is gong-focused, and the studio also runs certification training, so some of the people in the room may be there to learn the craft, not just receive it. Bechtold also runs mobile sessions, bringing the full setup to private homes, offices, or events on request. Best for first-timers who want a practice with a long track record and a serious, no-frills gong sound, and for anyone curious about eventually training as a practitioner. The volume of repeat sessions alone says something about consistency.

## Lemurian Water Sounds, in Venice

Larimar OceAna runs the most unusual format on this list: ceremonial sound baths on the beach and sound healing journeys held on a boat, out at sea. Crystal bowls are the main instrument, and their tones travel differently over open water than they do inside a studio, carried and softened by wind and tide. The boat format limits capacity by necessity, which keeps sessions small and intentional rather than crowded. VoyageLA profiled the practice, confirming both the practitioner and the neighborhood. Best for visitors or locals who want the ocean itself to be part of the session, not just scenery it happens near.

## Yogi Dave Sound Bath, in Tarzana, San Fernando Valley

This is one of the few LA listings with fully published pricing: $20 in advance, $25 day-of. Sessions run gong and crystal singing bowls on a recurring Sunday evening schedule at a fixed studio in Tarzana, which makes it easy to build into a routine rather than chase one-off events across the city. It holds a perfect rating so far, though from a small review base. Best for Valley residents who want a consistent, low-cost weekly practice without crossing the hill into central LA traffic every week. Book through Eventbrite.

## Well & Whole, in Westchester and Playa

Gaia's studio is one of the only practices in this guide with a full, published price ladder: classes and workshops from $35, private sessions from $124, day retreats from $244. The format combines yoga therapy, sound healing, and Reiki, so clients can build a session around energy work, movement, or sound depending on what they need on a given day. It holds a 5.0 rating across 19 reviews. Best for anyone on the Westside below the 405, an area with fewer dedicated studios than Santa Monica proper, who wants price transparency before booking anything at all.

## Sacred Knots, in Silver Lake

Sacred Knots is not a sound bath studio first. It is a structural bodywork practice built around Rolfing, Craniosacral Therapy, and Somatic Experiencing, with sound healing woven in as a complementary layer rather than the main event. That gives it a clinical grounding a standalone sound bath does not have: the sound work addresses the energetic side while the bodywork addresses fascia and tissue directly. Silver Lake's density supports this kind of practice, with walk-in traffic and neighborhood word-of-mouth doing a lot of the marketing on its own. Best for people managing physical tension who want sound folded into real bodywork instead of offered by itself.

## Getting the most from your LA sound bath

Book ahead, especially for the smaller or boat-based sessions listed here. Several of these practices, like Lemurian Water Sounds, cap attendance because of the venue itself, and slots go fast once a date is posted publicly. Ask about instrumentation before you book if you have a preference either way. Gong-heavy sessions like The Soundbath Center or Yogi Dave feel physically different from a crystal bowl set, more visceral and less melodic, and some people have a strong reaction one way or the other the first time. Arrive with a light stomach and comfortable layers you can move in. Studios rarely heat or cool the room much during a session, and lying still for 45 minutes gets cold fast, even in LA in the middle of summer. If you are new to the format entirely, start with a group session before booking anything private. It is cheaper, lower commitment, and gives you a real sense of what actually works for your body before you spend $150 or more on a one-on-one session.

Consider the neighborhood as much as the practitioner. A Silver Lake studio session, a Venice boat ride, and a Tarzana Sunday evening are three different experiences of the same basic idea, and none of them is the objectively correct way to do a sound bath. Pick based on what setting actually helps you relax, not just what has the best reviews on paper.
