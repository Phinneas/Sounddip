The city that never sleeps has a surprisingly hard time switching off — which is exactly why its sound bath scene is as dense as it is. New York has one of the deepest practitioner pools in the country, and unlike Los Angeles, where dedicated sound temples anchor the market, most of it runs through yoga studios, meditation centres, and small independent practitioners who move between borrowed rooms.

That makes it harder to find the real ones. The listings blur together. This guide picks seven specific sessions across Brooklyn and Manhattan that are worth your evening — what makes each distinctive, what it costs, and how to actually get there.

## Brooklyn Greenway Initiative Sound Baths

**Multiple locations, Brooklyn · Free · 60 minutes**

The outlier in this list, and arguably the best value in the city. Brooklyn Greenway Initiative runs outdoor sound baths at the Greenway Botanical Garden and Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6, using singing bowls, gong, harmonium, and voice. It is free, open to all, and first-come — you bring a mat or blanket and find a spot on the grass.

What makes it distinctive is the setting. An outdoor sound bath with the harbour behind you is a different experience from a darkened studio, and the fact that it costs nothing removes the usual commitment barrier. These run as scheduled events rather than a standing weekly slot, so check their Eventbrite calendar before you go.

**Getting there:** Brooklyn Bridge Park Pier 6 is a short walk from the 2/3 at Clark Street or the F at York Street. The Greenway Botanical Garden sits along the Columbia Street Waterfront, reachable via the F at Carroll Street.

## Marta Hernández — Weekly Deep Relaxation Sound Bath

**Williamsburg, Brooklyn · Paid via Eventbrite or Venmo · 330 Wythe Ave**

A recurring Monday night session at 7:30pm, run by practitioner Marta Hernández. Mixed bowls and gongs in a small room, and the weekly rhythm is the point: this is the kind of session you can build a habit around rather than treat as a one-off.

Williamsburg has no shortage of sound offerings, but most are programmed events attached to a studio's broader schedule. This one is a practitioner-led weekly, which usually means a more consistent arc from session to session. Price is listed as paid via Eventbrite or Venmo with the exact amount shown at booking.

**Getting there:** 330 Wythe Ave is a few minutes from the Bedford Avenue L, which puts it within easy reach of Union Square in about 15 minutes and downtown Brooklyn in about 20.

## OHM Center

**Upper East Side, Manhattan · $35–$50 per class · 45–60 minutes**

The most structured offering on this list. OHM Center runs group sound bath classes daily across multiple time slots, with crystal bowls and gongs while you lie on a mat under blankets. Packages are available if you want to commit to more than one.

This is the pick for anyone who wants predictability: set times, published pricing, a dedicated space. It is also the easiest Manhattan option to recommend to a first-timer who does not want to decode an Eventbrite listing. The Upper East Side location is straightforward from most of the borough.

**Getting there:** Upper East Side, reachable via the 4/5/6 at 86th Street. From Brooklyn, budget 40 to 50 minutes door to door.

## Surya Sound Temple

**Bushwick, Brooklyn · From $99 · 60 minutes**

The premium pick. Dan and Sarah play gongs, crystal bowls, and voice in a room that stays quiet throughout — you lie on a mat with a blanket and an eye pillow, and most people drift off. Sessions run about an hour and are billed as a Deep Meditation Sound Experience.

What makes this distinctive is the intensity and the production. Gong-led sessions hit harder physically than bowl-only ones, and this is the one on the list most likely to produce a genuine shift rather than a pleasant unwind. It is priced accordingly, starting at $99.

**Getting there:** Bushwick, reachable via the L at Jefferson Street or the M at Central Avenue. From Midtown, expect about 40 minutes.

## Windsong Sound Bath

**Fort Greene, Brooklyn · Price on contact · 60 minutes**

A private studio running small groups where you choose your own spot and lie down. Instruments vary each session, which means no two visits are identical — a deliberate choice that suits people who have already done a few standard bowl sessions and want something less formulaic.

The small-group format is the selling point. Fewer bodies in the room means the practitioner can place instruments closer and read the room's response. Booking and pricing are handled directly rather than through a ticketing platform, so contact them ahead of time.

**Getting there:** Fort Greene, near the B/Q/R at DeKalb Avenue or the G at Clinton-Washington. About 25 minutes from Union Square.

## My Little Magic Shop — Sound Bath for Expression and Voice

**West Side, Manhattan · 90 minutes**

The unusual one. This session uses crystal singing bowls and chimes alongside voice-focused guidance, and participants are invited to hum or tone along rather than stay silent. Ninety minutes, so it is a longer commitment than most on this list.

It is a good counterpoint for anyone who finds standard sound baths too passive. The vocal component makes it closer to a workshop than a passive listening session, and that changes the experience substantially. If you have any interest in how your own voice sits inside a soundscape, this is the one to book.

**Getting there:** West Side, Manhattan — reachable via the 1/2/3 along the Seventh Avenue line.

## Soundawn Sound Bath Center

**Williamsburg, Brooklyn · Private from booking page; corporate from $1,150/group**

Crystal bowls, gong, and chimes, 30 to 60 minutes, fully clothed and lying down. What distinguishes Soundawn is the range: they run private sessions in a Williamsburg studio as well as corporate bookings for groups up to 20, so the same practitioners work at both scales.

The corporate angle matters if you are booking for a team rather than yourself — it is one of the few listings in the city with published group pricing. For individuals, the private sessions are the draw: shorter options are available if a full hour is more than you want.

**Getting there:** Williamsburg, near the Bedford Avenue L or the J/M/Z at Marcy Avenue.

## Brooklyn vs Manhattan: the two cultures

The borough split is real and worth understanding before you book. Brooklyn's scene skews practitioner-led and informal — small rooms, recurring weekly slots, Eventbrite listings, and a strong presence in Williamsburg, Bushwick, and Fort Greene. Prices run lower, and the sessions tend toward the intimate.

Manhattan's offerings skew more institutional: dedicated centres like OHM, structured class schedules, published pricing, and venues that run sound as one part of a broader wellness menu. You pay more for the convenience and the reliability, and the rooms are usually larger.

Neither is better. If you want a habit you can keep, Brooklyn's weekly practitioner sessions are the stronger foundation. If you want a specific time slot on a specific evening with no surprises, Manhattan is easier.

## Getting there from the outer boroughs

Commuting is the real cost of a New York sound bath, and it is worth planning around. From **Queens**, most Brooklyn venues run 35 to 50 minutes via the E/M or the G, depending on where you start. From **the Bronx**, expect 45 to 60 minutes to either borough. From **Staten Island**, you are looking at an hour-plus in almost every case, and the ferry schedule is the constraint.

The practical rule: pick a session within 30 minutes of your door, or pick one close to where you already are that evening. A sound bath you arrive at flustered and late is a worse experience than one you can walk to.
