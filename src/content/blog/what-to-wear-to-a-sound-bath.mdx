Wear soft, comfortable clothing that lets you lie still for 45 to 90 minutes, and bring layers. That is the entire practical answer. Everything else is preference.

Most sound baths happen lying down on a mat or in a zero-gravity chair, often on the floor, often in a dim room. You are not moving much. You are not sweating much. The goal is to remove anything that pulls your attention back to your body: tight waistbands, scratchy tags, shoes, jewelry that clinks when you shift.

## What should you actually wear to a sound bath?

Loose layers work best. Think what you would wear to a restorative yoga class or a long flight where you want to sleep. Leggings, joggers, or soft pants. A t-shirt or long-sleeve top. A hoodie, cardigan, or wrap you can pull on or push off without sitting up.

Avoid jeans, belts, stiff collars, and anything with hardware that presses into your back when you lie down. Zippers, buttons, and thick seams are the usual culprits. If you have a favorite pair of pants for napping, those are probably the right ones.

## Should you bring socks or a blanket?

Yes to both, in most rooms. Body temperature drops when you lie still for an hour, and studios are often kept cool. Socks are the single most common thing people wish they had brought. Many venues provide blankets and bolsters, but not all do, and the ones they provide are sometimes thin. A small throw blanket and a pair of socks take up almost no space in a bag.

## What about jewelry, hair, and accessories?

Remove dangling earrings, bracelets, and necklaces before you lie down. Metal against a hardwood floor or a singing bowl's rim makes noise, and loose jewelry can dig into your neck or wrist when your head is turned. Hair ties are fine. If you have long hair, a loose braid or bun keeps it from getting pinned under your shoulders.

## Do you need special clothing for a sound bath?

No. There is no dress code, no required uniform, and no tradition that specifies particular garments. Sound baths draw from Tibetan singing bowl practices, gong meditation, and Western therapeutic sound work, and none of those lineages prescribe clothing for participants. Comfort is the only functional requirement.
